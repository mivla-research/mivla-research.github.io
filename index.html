MiVLA: Towards Generalizable Vision-Language-Action Model with Human-Robot Mutual Imitation Pre-training

Learning transferable robot behavior from human videos and simulated robot demonstrations.

02 / ABSTRACT

Two scalable sources. One action model.

Vision-language-action models are typically pretrained on robot demonstrations, but real-world robot data is costly and difficult to collect at scale. Simulated robot trajectories offer varied manipulation skills, while human videos capture everyday actions in real scenes. Each source has a transfer gap: simulation differs from reality, and human hands differ from robot arms.

MiVLA bridges the action spaces with bidirectional human–robot conversion. For each demonstration, the model learns both the observed embodiment’s action and a converted action for the other embodiment. This mutual imitation objective brings human and simulated robot behavior into one policy, which is then adapted to downstream robot tasks.

THE CENTRAL IDEAEvery demonstration teaches two behaviors: what happened, and how the other embodiment could do it.
03 / METHOD

Human and robot actions, aligned in both directions.

MiVLA combines a geometric action bridge with a shared diffusion-based action policy.

01

Human → robot

Align relative human hand motion to a robot end-effector target, then use inverse kinematics to recover robot joint actions.

02

Robot → human

Use the robot end-effector as a hand reference and estimate finger positions from anatomical priors.

03

Mutual imitation

Train on the demonstrated action and the converted cross-embodiment action for both kinds of observation.

POLICY INPUT

Image, language instruction, and proprioceptive state.

ACTION REPRESENTATION

Human joints, robot joints, and end-effector pose.

ACTION DECODER

A diffusion transformer trained for continuous action prediction.

04 / TRAINING RECIPE

What each data source contributes

A

Simulated robot demonstrations

Provide robot action trajectories and manipulation diversity, with a simulation-to-reality gap.

B

Human videos

Provide real-scene behavior and task variety, with a hand-to-robot embodiment gap.

↔

One shared pre-training objective

Both data types supervise original-action prediction and cross-embodiment imitation.

The paper’s “without real robot data” claim refers to pre-training. The real-robot experiments use 30 robot demonstrations per task for task-specific fine-tuning.

Where mutual imitation makes the difference

FEW-SHOT ADAPTATION · TABLE 4

Small task-specific datasets

With 30 bottle-task demonstrations, the bidirectional setting reaches 56% success, compared with 0% from scratch. The benefit varies with task and demonstration count.

GENERALIZATION · TABLE 5

Changed locations, objects, scenes

In the reported full-data setting, MiVLA reaches 75% on seen cases, 50% on unseen locations, 30% on unseen objects, and 38% on unseen scenes.

LIMITATIONS REPORTED BY THE AUTHORS

Novel object shapes, unusual or cluttered initial poses, and highly distracting backgrounds can still cause failures. In some challenging out-of-distribution cases, the paper observes stronger semantic generalization from π₀.₅.
